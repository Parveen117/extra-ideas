# CP1 — A self-similar centre leaves exactly one defect

Sources read: ON1 (defects = running of ε), UP1 (degree), UP6 (scale operations).

Centre potential with two weights: U(l^a S, l^b V) = l·U, i.e. U = S^{1/a} G(V/S^{b/a}), G free.
Scaling flow D = a·S∂_S + b·V∂_V. Readings T = U_S, P = −U_V, ratio ε = S P_S / (V P_V).

- **CP1-T0.** D U = U, D T = (1−a) T, D P = (1−b) P: every reading has a weight.
- **CP1-T1 (one defect).** a·(S∂_S ε) + b·(V∂_V ε) = 0. The two ON1 defects are tied in the fixed
  ratio −b : a. Of the two non-commutations only one is independent, and its direction reads the weights.
- **CP1-T2.** a·S P_S + b·V P_V = (1−b) P: the weights are readable from first responses alone.
- **CP1-T3.** The surviving defect is not zero in general (G = 1/(1+x)+x², a=2, b=3, S=1, V=2: −29/224).
  So a self-similar centre is *not* a commuting centre; it is a one-defect centre.
  (This corrects the guess recorded at the end of ON1.)
- **CP1-T4.** With a built-in scale (P = S/(V−1) + S²) the ratio of the two defects is not constant:
  no weights exist, both defects are independent.
- **CP1-T5.** T S / (P V) is invariant along the flow.

Ladder: commuting (ε constant, ON1-T2) ⊂ one defect (weights a, b) ⊂ two defects (scale present).

Put in: smooth U, two free directions, P_V ≠ 0. Not claimed: values of a, b for any substance;
that nature's critical points realise this form (that is the comparison to be made next).
