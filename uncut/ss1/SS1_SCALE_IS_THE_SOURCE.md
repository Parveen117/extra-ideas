# SS1 — The source of non-commutation is a scale

Sources read: ON1 (defect = running of ε), CP1 (self-similar centre: a·β_S + b·β_V = 0), DW1, PL2, XU3
(defect depends on x = h·r_b only), UP5-T5 (frame part and weight part), UP1 (degree).

Centre potential with one built-in scale b₀, covariant when state and scale are stretched together:
U(l^a S, l^b V; l^γ b₀) = l·U. ε = S·P_S/(V·P_V).

- **SS1-T1 (the law).** a·(S∂_S ε) + b·(V∂_V ε) = −γ·(b₀∂ε/∂b₀).
  The combination of defects that vanishes for a self-similar centre is exactly the response of ε to the scale.
- **SS1-T2.** No scale in the potential ⇒ CP1's tie. ε constant ⇒ ON1's commuting case.
- **SS1-T3.** ON1's witness P = S/(V − b₀): V∂_V ε = −b₀∂ε/∂b₀ — the defect is the scale response, and it
  vanishes with b₀.
- **SS1-T4 (outer scale).** XU3's defect depends on (r_b, h) only through h·r_b: the same law with the
  outer length 1/h as the scale.
- **SS1-T5.** With an inner and an outer scale the two responses add.

Together with UP5-T5: the frame part of a defect comes from a scale inside the potential (ON1, DW1); the
weight part from a scale outside it — the reader's unit, which an expanding space lowers (XU1–XU3). λ = 0,
the flat diagram, is the case with no scale inside and none outside.

Put in: covariance of the potential under joint stretching (this is what "b₀ is the only scale" means).
Not claimed: why nature has scales; which scales it has.
