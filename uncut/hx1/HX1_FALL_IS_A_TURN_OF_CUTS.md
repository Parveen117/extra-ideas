# HX1 — The fall is a turning of the radial cut toward the third cut; the horizon exchanges them

Sources read: WD2 (three cuts C₁ = K, C₂ = RK, C₃ = ιR; C₁C₂C₃ = ι), QT1 (inputs: horizon cycle read by the
turn; one unit per closed turn), QT2-T5 (frame cut κ = cosh ψ·K + sinh ψ·R, tanh ψ = fall speed), MO1/GB1.

G = C₁C₃, G² = −1. Turning the radial cut: C₁·Exp(aG) = cos a·C₁ + sin a·C₃.

- **HX1-T2 (the fall).** κ = C₁·Exp(−ιψ·G): the frame cut is the radial cut turned toward the third cut
  by the imaginary angle −ιψ. A boost is not a second kind of motion; it is this turn with imaginary angle.
- **HX1-T3 (the horizon).** Past the horizon the angle is π/2 − ιψ′: a real quarter turn has been added
  and κ = cosh ψ′·C₃ + ι sinh ψ′·C₁. The two cuts have exchanged places. This is QT2-T5's continuation, exactly.
- **HX1-T4 (the closed reading).** With a real angle the same generator closes: π/2 gives C₃, π gives −C₁,
  2π gives C₁.
- **HX1-T5 (one generator).** Open reading (fall, growth) and closed reading (period) are Exp of one
  generator G; their angles differ by the factor ι = C₁C₂C₃, the product of all three cuts.
- **HX1-T6.** The turned cut squares to 1 for every complex angle: it remains a cut all the way through.

What this removes: QT1's input (i). The horizon cycle is not "re-read by a turn" from outside; fall and
closing turn are the same Exp, and crossing the horizon is where the angle picks up its real part.

Put in: directions = cuts (FR1); the frame cut's form (GB1). Still put in: QT1's (ii), one unit per closed
turn. Not claimed: a derivation of the period P = 2π/κ in reading time from T4 (the angle is identified, the
clock that runs it is not).
