# WD1 — The boundary is a whole number of turns only in two and in three directions

Sources read: QT3 (count/area = c·(two turns/Ω)/(d−1)), QT1 (one unit per closed turn), MC1 (d = 2 runs as
log r: no scale), AL1 (degree (d−2)/(d−1)), MO1 (period law (dφ/dt)² = r_s/(2r³)), RMG11 (two-valued transport).

Ω_d = measure of the unit boundary in d directions; one turn = 2π.

- **WD1-T1.** Ω_d / turn = 1 at d = 2, = 2 at d = 3, and is not a ratio of whole numbers for any other d
  (checked exactly for d = 2…40).
- **WD1-T2 (why, for every d).** Ω_{2m}/turn = π^{m−1}/(m−1)!, Ω_{2m+1}/turn = 2^m·π^{m−1}/(2m−1)!!.
  The power of π is m−1: it is absent only for m = 1, i.e. d = 2 and d = 3.
- **WD1-T3.** d = 2 has no scale: the centre value does not depend on r_s and the degree is 0. No horizon count.
- **WD1-T4.** d = 3: the line's own period law gives M = r_s/2, so c = ½ is derived, not put in.
- **WD1-T5.** With one unit per full turn the horizon count is a ratio-of-whole-numbers share of its
  boundary only at d = 3, where it is ¼ (for any c that is itself such a ratio).

Statement: three is the only number of directions in which (a) a scale exists and (b) the boundary closes
in whole turns — two of them, the full return of a two-valued reading. Only there can the horizon count be
certified as an exact ratio.

Put in: one unit per full turn (QT1). T2's "not a ratio for m > 1" uses that no power of π is a ratio of
whole numbers (general knowledge, not a framework theorem); T1 is the framework-side exact check to d = 40.
Not claimed: that nature must have an exact-ratio count — that requirement is the selection principle
proposed here, not a derived law.
Open gate: derive the requirement — a closed reading must return in whole turns (QT2-T3/T4) — for the
boundary itself, from the recognition-return criterion.
