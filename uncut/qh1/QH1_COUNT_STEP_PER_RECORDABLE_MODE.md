# QH1 — Is the count of a horizon a whole number? The line had the answer; what today's stages add

Sources read: **WQ1** (a recordable mode has E = n·κ·ω; the count n is frame-independent), **QC1-T3**, **BC1**
(inside the disc a return is a turn, on the boundary an addition; "A COUNT OF THE HORIZON — NOT DERIVED";
"QUANTISATION LOCATED AT THE BOUNDARY ALONE — REFUSED"), **GW1** (waves of the frame are pairs with the same unit),
**SC1 / DC1** (physics line: the unit's value is free; one unit for frames that exchange), ST1, SM1, CK1 (open gate).

CK1 asked whether the phases per closed turn are whole numbers. BC1 already answers: no — the boundary sector
only adds, it has no period of its own, and nothing in the line makes the horizon's count whole. That stands.
What can be added with ST1:

- **QH1-T1.** One count of a recordable mode of far rate ω given to the centre changes the count by
  ΔS = 2π·ω/κ_h (κ_h the horizon rate). The value of the unit drops out.
- **QH1-T2.** In turns, ΔS/2π is the number of turns the mode makes during one closing turn of the horizon.
- **QH1-T3.** It is whole exactly when ω/κ_h is whole. A mode at the horizon's own rate adds ΔS = 2π; in three
  directions the boundary then grows by 8π unit areas.
- **QH1-T4.** A mode of local rate ω_loc at clock factor N is read far at ω_loc·N: its step goes to zero at the
  boundary — BC1-B3's "the steps close up".
- **QH1-T5.** For the neutral centre ΔS = 8π·M·ω, any real number.

So what is whole is the count n of each mode (WQ1); the horizon's count changes by a pure ratio of two rates
per mode count, and is not itself whole.

Put in: T = (unit)·κ_h per turn (ST1/SM1), which BC1 does not derive. Not claimed: equally spaced boundary
areas as a law — T3 is the special case of a mode at the horizon's own rate, and nothing here says such modes
are the only ones.
