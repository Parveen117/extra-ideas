# BH2 — The turn-parts of a rotating horizon

Sources read: BH1 (charged horizon), DW1 (w = running × reading), CP1, BL1-T2 (cycle factor 1 + 2w²).

Centre potential put in: M(S,J) = √(S/4π + πJ²/S). Pure number y = 4π²J²/S² (0 non-rotating,
1 extremal, T = 0). Readings T = M_S, Ω = M_J. Self-similar with weights (2,2).

- **BH2-T2.** ε_Ω = −(y+3)/2, ε_T = −(3y²+6y−1)/(2y(y+3)). Neither is constant: both corners carry a defect.
- **BH2-T3 (rotation corner).** w_rot/M = −y²/(2(1+y)): no pole, grows steadily, −¼ at the extremal end.
- **BH2-T4 (heat corner).** w_heat/M = y(y−1)(3y²+2y+3) / ((1+y)(3y²+6y−1)²).
- **BH2-T5 (same three-point shape as BH1).** Heat-corner turn: zero at y = 0, zero at y = 1 (T = 0),
  double pole at y = 2/√3 − 1 (J²/M⁴ = 2√3 − 3), one sign throughout.
- **BH2-T6 (difference from the charged centre).** At the extremal end the heat turn stops but the
  rotation turn does not: first-cycle factor there is 9/8. The charged extremal horizon is at rest (BH1);
  the rotating one is not.

Shape law so far (two centres): heat-corner turn of a horizon = zero at the neutral end, zero where
T = 0, one double pole between, no change of sense.
