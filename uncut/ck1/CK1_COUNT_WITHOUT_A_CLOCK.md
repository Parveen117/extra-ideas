# CK1 — The count of a horizon with no time variable: phases accumulated per closed turn

Sources read: FC4 (a reader's time is its own count; the closing turn is the product of the three cuts), ST1
(count = area swept in the (M, t) plane; T = 1/P), SM1 (the closing period is forced), AR1-T4 (energy × period is the
same for every reader), ME1-T2 (angles per closed turn Φ·P, Ω·P are reader-free), AL1 (degree k = (d−2)/(d−1)),
CP1 (one scale: weights), HD1, XU2, CL1 (time is a count of events).

- **CK1-T1.** Neutral centre, any d: S = k·(M·P). The count is the degree times the phase the centre accumulates
  during one closed turn. In three directions M·P = 2S.
- **CK1-T2 (two counts, no clock).** M·P = (phase of the centre per tick of the reader) × (ticks of the reader per
  closed turn). The product does not depend on the size of the tick, nor on which reader counts (AR1-T4).
- **CK1-T3 (three planes, three directions).** S = ½·[M·P − (Φ·P)·Q] − (Ω·P)·J.
- **CK1-T4 (turning, d = 4, 5, 6).** S = k·M·P − (Ω·P)·J.
- **CK1-T5 (expansion plane).** S = ½·P·[M − h²r³].
- **CK1-T6 (one statement).** Σ over planes of (degree of the reading in length) × (angle per closed turn) ×
  (reading) = 0, with the count's own angle per turn equal to 1 and M entering with degree d−2 on the other side.
  In three directions: M·P = 2S + 2(Ω·P)J + (Φ·P)Q.

Every quantity in T1–T6 is a pure number counted per closed turn: a phase, an angle, or a count. ST1's time
axis has been replaced by "per closed turn", and the closed turn is itself made of the three cuts (FC4-T6).
The chain for the count now contains no time variable.

Put in: P as the closing period (SM1) and the one-scale weights. T1–T6 are the first law and the scaling
relation of the centres already in the line, multiplied through by P; the stage's content is the form — nothing
but phases per closed turn — and T2's reading of M·P as a product of two counts.
Not claimed: that the phases are whole numbers; a rule that would make them so is the open gate (a count that
is a number of marks, HB1/CL1's Q, against S = k·M·P).
