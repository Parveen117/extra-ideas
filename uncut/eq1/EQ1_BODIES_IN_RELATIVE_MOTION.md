# EQ1 — Two bodies in relative motion: no common temperature, a temperature per channel, and a one-way circuit

Sources read: MT1 (dS = P′(dE′ − v·dp′); three readings), PL1, RT1-T5, LG1 (a gain needs two directions), SL1.

- **EQ1-T1.** Free exchange of energy and momentum at fixed totals: the count is stationary for every
  exchange exactly when the two bodies share the period and the velocity. Bodies in relative motion have no
  equilibrium.
- **EQ1-T2 (a temperature per channel).** If the exchange goes through packets carrying momentum u × energy,
  the moving body has one number for that channel: T(u) = T₀ / (γ(1 − v·u)).
  Forward T₀√((1+v)/(1−v)), backward T₀√((1−v)/(1+v)).
- **EQ1-T6.** The transverse channel u = 0 gives T₀/γ — MT1's fixed-momentum reading. The readings of MT1 are
  channels: none is "the" temperature.
- **EQ1-T3.** Same rest temperature, v = 3/5: forward the mover reads 2, backward ½. Energy flows from the
  mover through the forward channel and to the mover through the backward one, at once.
- **EQ1-T4 (the circuit).** Giving e₁ forward and taking e₂ backward raises the count of the pair by
  e₁/2 + e₂ and takes momentum e₁ + e₂ from the mover. Both channels raise the count; both slow the mover.
- **EQ1-T5.** The channel temperatures of two bodies agree for every u only if their velocities agree.

Answer, in the line's terms, to "which way does heat flow between moving bodies": it depends on the
channel, and the net effect of all channels is a loss of relative motion with a gain of count. The two
directions LG1 requires are here the two channels.

Put in: MT1's form; packets with |u| ≤ 1; in T3–T4 a body at rest with the same rest temperature.
T(u) coincides with the known direction-dependent temperature of a moving radiator (general knowledge, not
checked against the literature here). Not claimed: rates; any statement for bodies that are not uniform.
