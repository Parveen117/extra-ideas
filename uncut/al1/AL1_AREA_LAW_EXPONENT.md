# AL1 — The count of a one-scale horizon: the boundary measure, in every dimension

Sources read: MC1 (potential r^−(d−2)), MO1 (β² = r_s/r), GR1 (N² + m = 1; horizon = balanced share),
HB1 (the unit of a frame = even part of its tick), ID1 (sharing ratio 1 : −1/(d−1)), BH1/BH2, UP1 (degree).

Set-up. N² = 1 − (r_s/r)^{d−2}. Centre value fixed by the one scale: M = c·r_s^{d−2}.

- **AL1-T1.** Clock-rate gradient at the horizon: κ = ½ ∂_r N² = (d−2)/(2 r_s).
- **AL1-T2 (the count).** With the reading T = u·κ (u = unit of the frame) and dS = dM/T, count zero
  when there is no horizon:  S = 2c·r_s^{d−1} / ((d−1)·u).
  The count goes as r_s^{d−1} — the measure of the boundary, not of the inside — for every d.
- **AL1-T3 (degree).** The centre potential has degree k = (d−2)/(d−1) in the count, so
  S·T_S/T = −1/(d−1): the heat ratio of the horizon is the sharing ratio of ID1.
  Unit u and constant c do not enter k.
- **AL1-T4 (three directions).** d = 3, c = ½: S = area/(8π·u). S = area/4 ⇔ u = 1/(2π).
- **AL1-T5.** With that unit, M = √(S/π)/2: the centre used in BH1 is recovered, no longer put in.

What this moves: in BH1/BH2 the whole of "S = area/4" was an input. Now the power (boundary measure)
and the degree are derived from one scale and the rate at the horizon; the input has shrunk to one
pure number, the unit u, and the quarter is the statement u = 1/(2π) — one unit per full turn.

Put in: T = u·κ (the reading is the unit times the horizon rate); M = c·r_s^{d−2}; count zero at r_s = 0.
Not claimed: u = 1/(2π) is derived; HB1 gives the unit as a pure number of the tick, per what angle is open.
Open gate: TT1's cycle is a boost Exp(−2ηn); a statement that closes the boost at the horizon into
one full turn of ι would fix u = 1/(2π) inside the framework.
