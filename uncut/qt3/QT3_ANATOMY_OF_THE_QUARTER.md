# QT3 — What the quarter is made of

Sources read: AL1 (S = 2c·r^{d−1}/((d−1)u)), QT1 (unit per closed cycle), QT2 (quarter turn per walk past the
horizon), ID1 (sharing ratio 1/(d−1)), MO1 (β² = r_s/r, r_s = 2M), RMG11 (two-valued transport).

Ω = measure of the unit boundary; area = Ω·r^{d−1}.

- **QT3-T1.** count/area = 2c / ((d−1)·u·Ω).
- **QT3-T2 (refusal).** "One unit per walk" (quarter turn each) gives count = area/16. The quarter of the
  count is not the quarter turn of the walk. One unit per full turn — four walks — gives area/4.
- **QT3-T3.** With one unit per full turn: count/area = c · (two turns / Ω) · 1/(d−1).
- **QT3-T4 (three directions).** Ω = 4π is exactly two turns, so count/area = c/(d−1) = ½ · ½:
  one half is c (M = r_s/2, the square of the fall speed is twice the potential), the other half is
  ID1's sharing ratio 1/(d−1).
- **QT3-T5.** The quarter in every dimension ⇔ c = (d−1)·Ω/(16π).
- **QT3-T6 (uncut form).** With angle α at the centre in place of 2π: count/area = c·(2α/Ω)/(d−1).

Reading: the ¼ is a product of three things the line already has — the cut-square (½), the sharing of
one radial direction against d−1 transverse ones (½ at d = 3), and the boundary being two turns.

Put in: c = ½ at d = 3 (MO1's normalisation of r_s); one unit per full turn. Not claimed: T5's c for d ≠ 3
is derived; why the boundary of three directions is two turns (it is the same 4π as RMG11's full return —
noted, not proved to be the same fact).
Open gate: derive c(d) from MC1's flux through the boundary; then T5 is a test, not a condition.
