# NA1 — A plane whose turns do not commute: the quadratic term of the frame part

Sources read: ME1 (dθ = dk ∧ θ₀ + k·dθ₀), ME2 §3 (what is not yet superimposed), WD2 (the carrier holds exactly
three cuts; one eighth of the boundary carries one eighth of a turn), HX1, FR1/IN1 (the three cuts), RMG4
(non-abelian curvature, project docs).

A plane with three partners, one per cut: A = (ι/2)·Σ_c a^c·C_c, the a^c being one-forms on the base variables.

- **NA1-T1.** The three cuts close among themselves: [C_a, C_b] = 2ι·s·ε_abc·C_c with one sign s.
- **NA1-T2 (the quadratic term).** The frame part is dA + A∧A = (ι/2)·Σ_c f^c·C_c with
  f^c = da^c − s·ε_cab·a^a ∧ a^b.
  Each partner's defect is its own pair equation plus the product of the other two partners.
- **NA1-T3.** With one cut active the product vanishes and ME1's pair equation is all that is left: the
  commuting planes of ME1/ME2 are the one-cut case.
- **NA1-T4 (a change of reader is flat).** A = g⁻¹dg built from two turns about different cuts has pieces that
  do not commute, and still no frame part: the quadratic term cancels the pair term exactly.
  Non-commuting partners are not by themselves a defect.
- **NA1-T5 (the boundary).** Reading along a direction keeps one partner form, ½(1 − cos θ)dφ. Its frame part is
  half the area form; its flux over one eighth of the boundary is π/4 — WD2-T3's one-eighth turn — and over
  the whole boundary one full turn. WD2's product of readings and this frame part are the same number.
- **NA1-T6.** For m base variables such a plane carries 3·m(m−1)/2 frame conditions: 3, 9, 18 for m = 2, 3, 4.

With ME1: the master equation for any set of planes is

  dθ = dk ∧ θ₀ + k·(dθ₀ + θ₀ ∧ θ₀),

the last term being zero for planes whose turns commute.

## Reach, stated plainly

The form f^c = da^c − s·ε·a∧a is the known shape of a field with three non-commuting charges; here it is
obtained from the three cuts and tied to WD2's count of the boundary (T5), which is the stage's own content.
The carrier has room for three cuts only (WD2-T2), so this plane has three partners and no more: a plane with
eight partners needs a larger carrier, which is the Yang-Mills line's subject and is not attempted here.

Put in: partners as one-forms on base variables; directions = cuts. Not claimed: a dynamics for the partners
(what fixes a^c); the weak or the strong force.
