# SL1 — Unbiased histories raise the count

Sources read: GE2-T3 (Publications, generalized-euler-evolution: a symmetric step is the pair of turns ±v;
ledger h = ½Σw|v|²; additive; not a state function), BL1-T2 (exp(I₀) after one cycle = 1 + 2w², sinh η = w),
TT1 (tanh η = t/ρ), ST1 (count = πr² = 4πM²), XU3, LG1, NC1 (readings are scale-readings: steps in Log).

A history with no preferred direction: steps ±η in Log r, weights ½ and ½.

- **SL1-T1 (one step).** mean count / count = cosh 2η = 1 + 2 sinh²η, exactly. This is BL1-T2's first-cycle
  factor 1 + 2w² with w = sinh η: BL1's growth of the shared information and the mean growth of the count
  under one unbiased step are the same number.
- **SL1-T2 (rate against the ledger).** The step's ledger is η²/2. For small steps
  d Log(mean count)/dτ = 4; for a reading of degree n in r the rate is n². The count's rate is the square
  of its degree.
- **SL1-T3 (the gap).** mean count / count of the mean centre = 1 + tanh²η. With TT1, 1 + (t/ρ)².
  The excess over the count of the mean centre is the square of the turn ratio.
- **SL1-T4 (exchange at fixed total).** Two centres exchanging ±δ with M₁ + M₂ fixed: the mean count rises
  by 8πδ², whatever M₁, M₂. Equal centres are the least count at fixed total, not the greatest.
- **SL1-T5 (expanding space).** For the frame-consistent count at fixed h the same drift is positive at
  every sampled x in (0, 1/√3) and tends to the flat value as x → 0.
- **SL1-T6.** In two directions only the symmetric part of the count form enters the mean. XU3's circuit
  part (the antisymmetric part) averages to zero over unbiased histories.

What this settles from the synthesis's open list:
- "A process that raises the count, and why it is the natural one": the process is any history without a
  preferred direction (GE2's symmetric steps). No sense has to be chosen; the count rises in the mean
  because it is convex in the scale. XU3's selected sense is not what drives it (T6).
- "BL1's growth and the count as one statement": T1.

Put in: steps are unbiased in Log r (the scale-reading chart of the uncut line); T1–T3 let the centre value
fluctuate, so its mean rises too (rate 1) — T4 is the statement at fixed total. T5 is sampled, not proved.
Not claimed: that the mean over histories is what one history does; a rate in physical time (τ is GE2's
ledger, not seconds); the observed second law.
Open gate: T3's gap 1 + tanh²η against GR1's N² + m = 1 — whether the excess count is the memory m.
