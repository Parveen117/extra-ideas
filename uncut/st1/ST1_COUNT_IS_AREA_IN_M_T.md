# ST1 — The count of a horizon is the area its closed turns sweep in the (M, t) plane

Sources read: UN2 (inputs left: (a) T = one unit per closed turn; (c) directions = cuts), HX1-T5 (open and
closed readings: one generator, angles differ by ι), BL1/CI1 (shared information I, exp(I₀) = cosh ψ),
HB1 (count against response, [N, P] = ιC), AL1, BH1.

- **ST1-T1 (open reading).** Along the orbit the shared information is I = Log cosh(κt); its rate tends to κ.
  κ is the shared information added per unit of far time.
- **ST1-T2 (closed reading).** The same generator with the real angle returns after P = 2π/κ.
- **ST1-T3 (the identity, three directions).** P = 8πM, and the area swept in the (M, t) plane as the centre
  value grows from 0 to M is ∫P dM = 4πM² = (boundary area)/4.
- **ST1-T4.** If the count is defined as that area per unit turn, then T = dM/d(count) = 1/P follows.
  Input (a) is no longer a statement about T; it is the definition of what is counted.
- **ST1-T5.** In every d the same area reproduces AL1's count with u = 1/(2π).
- **ST1-T6 (charged centre).** T = 0 at the extremal end, where M = Q. At fixed charge the (M, t)-area starts
  from zero there, so it gives the count above the extremal end's own πQ² (this is the first law restated,
  not an independent check); the πQ² itself has to come from the charge direction.

What is new here: a plane made of a thermodynamic reading (M) and the clock (t) has an area, and that area
equals a quarter of the boundary of space. The thermodynamic plane and the boundary measure the same thing.

Put in: (c) directions = cuts; the count defined as (M, t)-area per unit turn. Not claimed: that this
definition is the only possible one; a count for the extremal end.
Open gate: T6 — find the plane whose area is the extremal count πQ² (candidate: (Q, conjugate angle of Φ)),
so that the whole count is area in thermodynamic planes with no convention for the zero.
