### R35.1 — The complementary signed carrier derives a gapless envelope wave

\[
\chi(x,y)=(-1)^{\lfloor x/2\rfloor+\lfloor y/2\rfloor},
\qquad u_n=(-1)^n\chi\Psi_n,\qquad \Psi_{n+1}=B\Psi_n.

envelope evolves under \(Z=-\chi B\chi\). Put
\(d_i=S_i-I\), \(e_i=S_i+I\), \(\lambda_i=d_i^\dagger d_i\).
Then

\[
\boxed{Z+Z^\dagger=2I-L_*,\quad
L_*=\tfrac12(\lambda_x+\lambda_y)-\tfrac1{16}\lambda_x\lambda_y,}
\tag{35.2}
\]
\[
\boxed{u_{n+1}-2u_n+u_{n-1}=-L_*u_n,\qquad
(Z-I)^\dagger(Z-I)=L_*.}
\tag{35.3}
\]

It has the exact positive decompositions

\[
L_*=\tfrac38(d_x^\dagger d_x+d_y^\dagger d_y)
 +\tfrac1{32}\{(d_xe_y)^\dagger(d_xe_y)
                      +(d_ye_x)^\dagger(d_ye_x)\},
\tag{35.4}
\]
\[
3I-L_*=\tfrac14(e_x^\dagger e_x+e_y^\dagger e_y)
          +\tfrac1{16}(e_xe_y)^\dagger(e_xe_y).
\tag{35.5}

\(d_xu=d_yu=0\) on finite periodic component matching domains, with

**Proof.** Address counting gives \(\chi S_i\chi=-S_i\). Hence
\(\chi\lambda_i\chi=4I-\lambda_i\), and

Positivity forces both d differences to vanish when L has zero quadratic

### R35.2 — The source itself supplies two isotropic first-order role generators

Write coarse addresses as \((2a+\epsilon,2b+\eta)\), with
\(\epsilon,\eta\in\{0,1\}\). Retain these four parity marks and
the four source/record roles. This is a bijective regrouping into sixteen
roles at each component address (a,b), not a projection of the source.
In these coordinates Z commutes with the component shifts S_x,S_y and
is a sixteen-role Laurent matrix supported in \(\{-1,0,1\}^2\).

Denote it by \(Z(s,t)\), where s,t are formal shift markers. Direct
native multiplication yields

\[
Z(1,1)=I_{16},\qquad
Z(1+p,1+q)=I_{16}+A_xp+A_yq\pmod{(p,q)^2},
\]
\[
\boxed{A_i^\dagger=A_i,\quad A_x^2=A_y^2=I_{16}/2,
\quad A_xA_y+A_yA_x=0.}
\tag{35.6}
\]

Consequently the positive leading count form is \(\operatorname{diag}(1/2,1/2)\),
of rank two. It differs from R31's bare-target form
\(\tfrac12\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\).
This repairs that degeneracy **in the different retained interface**; it
does not change R31's theorem or derive three physical spatial dimensions.

**Proof.** Write \(B=\sum B_{ij}T_x^iT_y^j\), obtained by multiplying
(35.0). An output parity (epsilon,eta) receives input parity
\((\epsilon-i,\eta-j)\bmod2\) and component displacement

\[
(a',b')=\left(-\lfloor(\epsilon-i)/2\rfloor,
              -\lfloor(\eta-j)/2\rfloor\right).
\]

Its Z coefficient is \(-(-1)^{a'+b'}B_{ij}\). This finite rule
constructs all nine sixteen-role coefficients from the source and proves
the regrouping and support claims. At s=t=1, (35.3) gives
\((Z(1,1)-I)^\dagger(Z(1,1)-I)=0\); positivity therefore gives identity.
The first derivatives are explicitly the finite sums
\(A_x=\sum a'Z_{a'b'}\), \(A_y=\sum b'Z_{a'b'}\).
The certificate checks every entry from this rule.

There is an independent structural proof of their products. Under dagger,
\((1+p)^{-1}=1-p+p^2\) modulo degree three. The first-order terms of
\(Z^\dagger Z=I\) give self-dagger A_i. Put
\(K_1=A_xp+A_yq\). Since \(Z^{-1}=I-K_1+K_1^2-K_2\)
through degree two, \(Z+Z^{-1}=2I+K_1^2\) there. But
\(\lambda_x=-p^2\), \(\lambda_y=-q^2\) through this degree.
(35.2) therefore gives \(K_1^2=(p^2+q^2)I/2\).
Comparing the three independent monomials proves (35.6). These are finite
cut-count jets, not an imported differential equation. For any radial
\(k=(k_x,k_y)\), \(A(k)=k_xA_x+k_yA_y\) thus obeys
\(A(k)^2=(k_x^2+k_y^2)I/2\). The positive root exists by R16 C8;
this proves the leading slopes \(\pm|k|/\sqrt2\).

### R35.3 — Native phase probes give two actual source bands without a Fourier premise

\(z_i=\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(\iota q_i)\), with native radial

Equivalently the unwrapped field has phase profile \(z_x^{-a}z_y^{-b}v\)
with sixteen-role coefficient v; no finite global norm is assigned to it.

Set

\[
r=(1-\operatorname{Cos}_\Sigma q_x)/2,\quad
s=(1-\operatorname{Cos}_\Sigma q_y)/2,\quad
\ell=2(r+s)-rs,\qquad 0\le r,s\le1.
\]

Then \(0\le\ell\le3\). The exact source phase roots are

\[
\boxed{\zeta_\pm=1-\ell/2\ \pm\ \frac{\iota}{2}
                                  \sqrt{\ell(4-\ell)}.}
\tag{35.7}
\]

For \(\ell>0\), both roots occur, with eight coefficient directions
each. Their projectors are constructed by finite division:

\[
P_\pm=\frac{Z(z_x,z_y)-\zeta_\mp I}{\zeta_\pm-\zeta_\mp},
\quad P_++P_-=I,\quad P_+P_-=0,\quad ZP_\pm=\zeta_\pm P_\pm.
\tag{35.8}
\]

At \(r=s=0\), Z is identity. Thus the phase relation is actually
realized by the first-order source; it is not an extra scalar wave family.
The multiplicity eight counts the retained parity/source/record marks,
not eight physical particles or polarizations.

**Proof.** F00-E's native unit-circle identity gives \(0\le r,s\le1\)
and substitutes \(\lambda_x=4r,\lambda_y=4s\) into (35.2), giving
ell. The same finite identities give unitary Z and
\(Z^2-(2-\ell)Z+I=0\). Completing this scalar square gives (35.7),
using R16's positive root. The roots have unit norm, are distinct for
ell>0, and multiplication of the quadratic proves all of (35.8).

The source coefficient rule in R35.2 gives the full trace, coefficient
by coefficient:

\[
\operatorname{tr}Z(s,t)=2+3(s+s^{-1}+t+t^{-1})
       +\tfrac12(s+s^{-1})(t+t^{-1})=8(2-L_*(s,t)).
\]

Hence \(\operatorname{tr}P_\pm=8\). An idempotent decomposes a finite
native ledger into its image and kernel: \(v=Pv+(I-P)v\), with zero
intersection. Choosing bases of these two spaces makes its diagonal I,0;
finite \(\operatorname{tr}(AB)=\operatorname{tr}(BA)\) preserves trace
under that change. Thus trace equals rank and both projectors have rank
eight. At ell=0, the evaluated matching identity
\((Z-I)^\dagger(Z-I)=0\) instead forces Z=I. No spectral theorem,
ordinary complex starting field or quantum-measurement axiom is imported.

### R35.4 — The complete phase family has a sharp native speed bound

For ell>0 define the local phase differential by
\(d\omega=\operatorname{turn}(\zeta_+^\dagger d\zeta_+)\).
The other branch reverses this differential. This definition uses the
already derived orientation and pairing. Locally it is the Euler phase
derivative; it does not need a global argument branch or an imported pi.
Its two count slopes are

\[
v_x=\frac{(2-s)\operatorname{Sin}_\Sigma q_x}
                       {2\sqrt{\ell(4-\ell)}},\qquad
v_y=\frac{(2-r)\operatorname{Sin}_\Sigma q_y}
                       {2\sqrt{\ell(4-\ell)}}.
\tag{35.9}
\]

They satisfy the sharp uniform bound

\[
\boxed{v_x^2+v_y^2\le\tfrac12,\qquad
\sup |v|=c_\Sigma=1/\sqrt2.}
\tag{35.10}
\]

For every nonzero radial direction k, the limit along q=epsilon k
as epsilon decreases to zero has magnitude \(1/\sqrt2\) and direction
k. Equality is a small-phase limit; regular nonzero ell has strict inequality.
This is a theorem about phase-gradient slopes. Wave-packet asymptotics and
an exact finite-support signal cone are separate questions.

**Proof.** Write \(c=1-\ell/2\), \(h=\sqrt{1-c^2}>0\), so
\(\zeta_+=c+\iota h\). Differentiating its native square gives
\(dh=-c\,dc/h\), and its turn differential is
\(c\,dh-h\,dc=-dc/h=d\ell/\sqrt{\ell(4-\ell)}\).
F00-E gives \(dr=\operatorname{Sin}_\Sigma(q_x)dq_x/2\) and its y
counterpart, proving (35.9).

Since \(\operatorname{Sin}_\Sigma^2q_x=4r(1-r)\), their squared
sum is \(N/[\ell(4-\ell)]\), where
\(N=(2-s)^2r(1-r)+(2-r)^2s(1-s)\). Direct expansion gives

\[
\boxed{\tfrac12\ell(4-\ell)-N
 =2r^2(1-s)+2s^2(1-r)+rs(2-r-s)+\tfrac32r^2s^2\ge0.}
\tag{35.11}
\]

Every displayed term is nonnegative on the native square 0<=r,s<=1.
Their sum vanishes only when r=s=0, proving the bound and strictness.
The native factorial series gives
\(r=\epsilon^2k_x^2/4+O(\epsilon^4)\), its s counterpart and
\(\ell=\epsilon^2|k|^2/2+O(\epsilon^4)\). Substitution in (35.9)
gives \(v\to k/(\sqrt2|k|)\). The factorial tails justify these
native limits. Along s=0 one has exactly
\(|v|^2=(1-r)/(2-r)\); its deficit from one half is
\(r/[2(2-r)]\), an independent rational sharpness check.

### R35.5 — A finite-product bound controls the long-scale phase dynamics

Let \(K=|k_x|+|k_y|\), \(A(k)=k_xA_x+k_yA_y\), and
\(Z_\epsilon=Z(\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(\iota\epsilon k_x),
\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(\iota\epsilon k_y))\). For
\(0\le\epsilon K\le1/2\), and every integer n>=0,

\[
\boxed{\|Z_\epsilon^n-
        \operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(\iota n\epsilon A(k))\|
                \le89n\epsilon^2K^2.}
\tag{35.12}
\]

Norm means the finite native matching bound, also extended to the derived
turn-valued coefficients. The leading phase flow is explicit. For
\(a=|k|/\sqrt2>0\),

\[
\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(\iota t A(k))
 =\operatorname{Cos}_\Sigma(at)I
  +\iota\operatorname{Sin}_\Sigma(at)A(k)/a.
\tag{35.13}
\]

For fixed t>=0, epsilon>0 and \(n=\lfloor t/\epsilon\rfloor\), (35.12) and the
vanishing event-rounding error prove modewise convergence to (35.13).
This is a controlled finite-mode limit. It does not claim a completed
Fourier transform, continuum convergence for every finite-energy field,
or physical Lorentz invariance.

**Proof.** For a native unit-phase parameter h with |h|<=1, the factorial
tail gives \(|\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(\iota h)-1-\iota h|\le h^2\):
use \(n!\ge2^{n-1}\) for n>=2 to bound the sum of reciprocal
factorials by one. For the nine source coefficients in R35.2, put
\(m(M)=\sum_{ij}|M_{ij}|\). Each matching matrix unit has norm one;
the triangle inequality therefore bounds an operator by m(M). Finite
source multiplication gives the exact rational ledger

\[
\sum_{a',b'}m(Z_{a'b'})(|a'|+|b'|)^2=88.
\tag{35.14}
\]

The coefficient rule displayed in R35.2 defines this finite sum completely;
the certificate checks all entries and its value, rather than sampling phases.
Every displacement has total size at most two. Apply the factorial bound
to \(h=\epsilon(a'k_x+b'k_y)\), sum it with (35.14), and use the
exact first jet. This gives
\(\|Z_\epsilon-I-\iota\epsilon A(k)\|\le88\epsilon^2K^2\).

By (35.6), \(A(k)^\dagger A(k)=a^2I\), so \(\|A(k)\|=a\le K\).
Its matrix factorial series is Cauchy by the same majorant; the identical
remainder argument costs at most \(\epsilon^2K^2\). Termwise dagger
and the finite binomial identity for commuting powers give its inverse
and pairing preservation. Thus
\(\|Z_\epsilon-\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(\iota\epsilon A(k))\|
\le89\epsilon^2K^2\).
Expand the difference of n-th powers as n ordered terms, each with this
middle difference and pairing-preserving outer factors. The triangle
inequality proves (35.12). Even and odd powers of A(k) give (35.13)
directly. Finally \(|n\epsilon-t|<\epsilon\) and the same factorial
tail makes its phase-flow error tend to zero. No classical limit or
spectral theorem is used in this proof.
