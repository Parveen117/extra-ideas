# R45: native curvature flow, directed transfer and phase-direction recovery

Research owner: Monty Dabas. Development: 2 October 2026 (India).

R44 derived ordering curvature from overlapping exchanges. R45 constructs
an actual flow generated by that curvature, using only native exchange
arrows, their inverses, counted words and the earned completion. A balanced
eight-factor word has a fully retained finite correction and converges to
the curvature flow with an explicit inverse-count bound.

On a cyclic single-cut target, the summed curvature factors exactly into
an oriented response and the R44 exchange-cost operator. In fact the two
operators together reconstruct an oriented shift:

\[
\boxed{\Omega_N=D_NG_N,\qquad
 T^{-1}=I-G_N+\iota\Omega_NG_N^{\#}.}
\tag{45.A}
\]

The inverse marked by # is constructed below on the nonzero-cost cut.
It is not a primitive generalized inverse. The cyclic carrier and the
orientation of the exchange words are explicit native target choices.
No material field equation, physical clock, ordinary spectral theorem,
control-limit theorem or classical graph operator is assumed.

## Native inputs and target

Take two R43 swaps S,T on two edges sharing one ledger. Their native
tuple action gives S-square=T-square=I and X=ST with X-cube=I. Set

\[
 P=(I-S)/2,\quad Q=(I-T)/2,\quad
 \Omega=\iota[P,Q]=\frac{\iota}{4}(X-X^{-1}),\quad
 P_c=I-\frac{I+X+X^{-1}}3,\quad P_0=I-P_c.
\tag{45.1}
\]

R44 proves that P_c is a self-dagger cut, Omega P_0=0 and
`Omega^2=(3/16)P_c`. These identities lift by spectator tuple marks.
All norms below are gain bounds or finite coefficient sums in the native
matching. All readouts are normalized native quadratic readouts.

The scalar x below parametrizes an already available native rational
exchange arrow. The later parameter t is accumulated quadratic loop
count, explicitly `t=2 n x^2`. Neither is identified with physical time
or with the duration of a primitive control factor.

## Written results

### R45.1 — An eight-factor native loop isolates curvature with an exact finite residue

The native inverse construction of R42/R44 gives

\[
 C_S(x)=(I+\iota xP/2)^{-1}(I-\iota xP/2)
       =\frac{I+\iota xS/2}{1+\iota x/2}.
\tag{45.2}
\]

Define C_T similarly, and retain the ordered words

\[
 L(x)=C_S(x)C_T(x)C_S(-x)C_T(-x),\qquad
 B(x)=L(x)L(-x).
\tag{45.3}
\]

Every factor and word preserves matching. The balanced word has no
linear or cubic term and satisfies the exact identity

\[
\boxed{B(x)=I+2\iota x^2\Omega+
 \frac{\sum_{j\in\{4,5,6,7,8,10\}}x^jR_j}
      {(1+x^2/4)^4}.}
\tag{45.4}
\]

The residue coefficients are native finite words:

| j | R_j |
|---|---|
| 4 | `(-4I-TS+5ST)/16` |
| 5 | `iota(-S-T+2STS)/16` |
| 6 | `(-4I-9TS+13ST)/64` |
| 7 | `iota(-S-T+2STS)/128` |
| 8 | `(-I-8TS+9ST)/256` |
| 10 | `(ST-TS)/512` |

Consequently, for |x|<=1,

\[
\boxed{\|B(x)-I-2\iota x^2\Omega\|
       \le\frac{355}{256}|x|^4.}
\tag{45.5}
\]

**Proof.** Since S-square=I, multiplication of the numerator of C_S by
its dagger gives `(1+x^2/4)I`, exactly the scalar denominator norm square.
This proves matching preservation and `C_S(-x)=C_S(x)^(-1)` directly.
The cut expression in (45.2) follows by splitting I into P and I-P.

Multiply the eight linear numerators in the order
`S+,T+,S-,T-,S-,T-,S+,T+`, where a sign denotes the sign of x in
`I+iota xS/2` or `I+iota xT/2`. Their scalar denominator is
`(1+x^2/4)^4`. Native tuple multiplication reduces each resulting word
to one of I,S,T,ST,TS,STS; their products can be checked on the three
ledger labels. Subtract the denominator times `I+2 iota x^2 Omega`.
Collecting equal powers gives exactly the displayed table, including
the zero coefficients in degrees below four. This is a finite source-word
expansion, not a truncated identification of the finite word with its
leading generator.

Every permutation word has gain one. The sums of coefficient norms in
the six rows are respectively `5/8,1/4,13/32,1/32,9/128,1/256`; their sum
is 355/256. The denominator is at least one and |x|^j<=|x|^4 for j>=4
in the declared range. This proves (45.5).

P_c commutes with S and T: conjugation interchanges X and X-inverse.
Thus every factor preserves the circulation cut and Omega-square.
The finite B(x) need not preserve Omega itself. On the single-cut
three-ledger sector, x=1 gives
`||[B(1),Omega]||_F^2=4374/390625`, a nonzero exact witness.
For another finite check, the unbalanced half-exchange loop L(2) has
`(L(2)+L(2)^dagger)/2=I-(3/8)P_c`; its turn contrast squares to
`(39/64)P_c`. Its retained finite phase is not replaced by a pure
curvature exponential.

### R45.2 — Counted balanced loops derive a pure curvature flow and its native cycle cuts

For t>=0 take `x_n=sqrt(t/(2n))`, with n>=t/2. Native count completion
gives

\[
\boxed{\lim_{n\to\infty}B(x_n)^n
 =U_c(t)=\operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(\iota t\Omega),\qquad
 \|B(x_n)^n-U_c(t)\|\le\frac{643}{1024}\frac{t^2}{n}.}
\tag{45.6}
\]

Negative t uses the inverse counted word. The phase generator relative
to this quadratic loop parameter is `E_c=-Omega`.

Construct the positive native scalar rho with rho-square=3 and put
r=rho/4. The two oriented cycle cuts and the complete flow are

\[
 P_\pm=\tfrac12(P_c\pm\Omega/r),\qquad
\boxed{U_c(t)=P_0+\cos_\Sigma(rt)P_c
                  +\frac{\iota}{r}\sin_\Sigma(rt)\Omega.}
\tag{45.7}
\]

P_0,P_+,P_- are mutually orthogonal native cuts; their phase rates are
0,+r,-r for U_c. The native cycle phase period is `8 theta_-/rho`.

**Proof.** From R44's cut square, the gain of Omega is at most r<1/2.
The factorial tail after the linear term of `Exp_Sigma(2 iota x^2 Omega)`
is at most `2 r^2 x^4 Exp_Sigma(2r x^2)`. For |x|<=1 this is at most
`(9/8)x^4`. Here `Exp_Sigma(1)<3` follows from its earned count series:
the first three terms sum to 5/2, and the remaining tail is at most
`(1/6)/(1-1/4)=2/9`. No imported exponential inequality is needed.
Together with (45.5), the one-step error is at most `(643/256)x^4`.
Telescope the difference of nth powers. All exact factors have gain one,
and the comparison exponentials obey the R42 factorial addition law.
Since n x_n^4=t^2/(4n), this proves (45.6).

The square roots used here are constructed within native completion.
For rho, start with [1,2], retain at each bisection the half interval
whose endpoint squares enclose 3, and use its count-Cauchy limit.
Lengths tend to zero, multiplication is continuous, and positive-square
ordering gives a unique positive limit with square 3. The same procedure
constructs the nonnegative roots in x_n; rational square-count subfamilies
are used for exact executable approximants.

The identity Omega-square=r-square P_c proves the cut identities of
P_+,P_- by multiplication. In the factorial series, the even powers of
Omega are `r^(2j)P_c` for j>=1 and the odd powers are `r^(2j)Omega`.
Grouping those earned, bounded series proves (45.7). The native circle
has period 2 theta_-, giving the stated phase period on the cycle cut.
No diagonalization or ordinary spectral theorem is a premise.

The flow exactly conserves Omega, P_c and every identical-readout sum.
The finite approximants exactly conserve P_c and those sums; Omega
conservation is a property of the completed flow, not an assertion that
the nonzero finite residue in R45.1 vanishes.

### R45.3 — Curvature generates oriented transfer, local pair memory and a delayed directional count signal

On three two-role ledgers, start with one Q mark at a. Put
`b=X^(-1)a`, `c=Xa`, phi=rt, and write c_phi,s_phi for the native cosine
and sine. In the ordered single-cut basis a,b,c, (45.7) gives

\[
\boxed{u_a=\frac{1+2c_\phi}{3},\quad
 u_b=\frac{1-c_\phi}{3}+\frac{s_\phi}{\rho},\quad
 u_c=\frac{1-c_\phi}{3}-\frac{s_\phi}{\rho}.}
\tag{45.8}
\]

The normalized local Q readings n_j=u_j^2 sum to one. At
`t_*=8 theta_-/(3 rho)`, the complete arrow is exactly X-inverse:
the retained cut transfers from a to b. Reversing the curvature flow
reverses this orientation.

Its directional count contrast is

\[
\boxed{n_b-n_c=\frac{4(1-\cos_\Sigma\phi)\sin_\Sigma\phi}{3\rho}
       =\frac{t^3}{32}-\frac{3t^5}{2048}+O(|t|^7).}
\tag{45.9}
\]

Thus the first direction-sensitive local count coefficient is cubic,
even though the amplitude response starts at first order.

Matching out the other ledgers gives the local pair
`rho_j=diag(1-n_j,n_j)`, with deficit `M_j=2n_j(1-n_j)`. At the cycle
reflection `t_R=4 theta_-/rho`, the exact counts and deficits are

\[
 (n_a,n_b,n_c)=(1/9,4/9,4/9),\qquad
 (M_a,M_b,M_c)=(16/81,40/81,40/81).
\tag{45.10}
\]

For this orbit the native response metric is exactly 1/8. More generally,

\[
\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(\Omega)
       =\frac{3}{16}\langle P_c\rangle-\langle\Omega\rangle^2.}
\tag{45.11}
\]

**Proof.** On the single-cut sector, the X-fixed cut is the all-ones
matrix divided by three. Also `iota Omega=(X^(-1)-X)/4`.
Insert these into (45.7) to obtain the three real coefficients (45.8).
Their squared sum is one by matching preservation, or by direct expansion
using rho-square=3 and the native circle identity.

At phi=2 theta_-/3 the native cubic root has radial coordinate -1/2
and positive turn coordinate rho/2: its sum with its inverse is -1,
by the finite identity for a nontrivial third root, and its norm is one.
Substitution in (45.7) or (45.8) gives U_c=X-inverse. At phi=theta_-
the coefficients are -1/3,2/3,2/3, proving the reflection counts.

Subtract the two neighbour squares in (45.8). The native factorial
coefficients and r=rho/4 give (45.9), with a controlled seventh-order
tail on bounded intervals. There is no inference of direction from a
discarded amplitude sign. The local pair formula follows by matching
the actual other-ledger marks: the one-cut and zero-cut alternatives
have different complementary marks, so their local cross term vanishes.
Squaring that pair gives its stated deficit, without a random-selection
or physical entropy postulate.

Finally use Omega-square=(3/16)P_c in the definition of variance and
R42's derived orbit metric. A basis mark has `<P_c>=2/3` and
`<Omega>=0`, both conserved by U_c. This gives 1/8 for the entire orbit.
The positive curvature activity `<Omega^2>` and the signed phase
readout `<Omega>` are distinct native quantities; neither is identified
as a material energy law here.

### R45.4 — Local curvature loops construct a collective flow and factor the cyclic response

For a specified finite ordered list of M overlapping-edge pairs, let
Omega_j and B_j be their native curvature and balanced words. Define
`Omega_Gamma=sum_j Omega_j`. With x_n as in R45.2,

\[
\boxed{\left(\prod_j B_j(x_n)\right)^n
 \longrightarrow \operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma(\iota t\Omega_\Gamma).}
\tag{45.12}
\]

For t>=0 and n>=t/2 an explicit gain error is

\[
\boxed{\frac{t^2}{n}\left[
 \frac{643M}{1024}+\frac{M^2}{4}
 \operatorname{Exp}_\Sigma\!\left(\frac{Mt}{2n}\right)\right].}
\tag{45.13}
\]

The construction counts 8Mn derived exchange factors. The list and its
orientation are declared targets; no physical handedness is selected.

For a ring of N>=3 source-role ledgers, orient consecutive edges and set
`Omega_N=sum_j iota[P_(j,j+1),P_(j+1,j+2)]`, with cyclic indices.
Restrict to one Q mark and let T|j>=|j+1>. Native multiplication gives

\[
 G_N=I-(T+T^{-1})/2,\qquad D_N=\iota(T-T^{-1})/2,
\]
\[
\boxed{\Omega_N=D_NG_N
 =\frac{\iota}{4}\big[2(T-T^{-1})-(T^2-T^{-2})\big].}
\tag{45.14}
\]

G_N is precisely the R44 single-cut generator divided by theta_-.
All three displayed operators commute on this cyclic target.

**Proof.** Replace each B_j(x_n) by `Exp_Sigma(iota(t/n)Omega_j)` using
R45.2's one-step bound, and telescope across the M factors. This costs
at most `(643M/1024)(t/n)^2`. The finite native factorial argument in
R44.1, with each local curvature gain at most 1/2, bounds the difference
between the product of exponentials and the exponential of their sum
by `M^2(t/n)^2 Exp_Sigma(Mt/(2n))/4`. Telescoping the nth powers proves
(45.13) and the completed flow. Identical-readout sums commute with
each constituent cut and therefore with the curvature and its flow.

On the one-cut sector, R44 gives
`P_(j,j+1)=(|j>-|j+1>)(<j|-<j+1|)/2`. Multiply two adjacent cuts,
subtract the reverse product, and sum over j. Each oriented nearest
edge occurs twice and each oriented two-edge displacement once with
the opposite sign. This gives the right side of (45.14). Multiplying
D_N G_N gives the same expression directly. All are finite Laurent
words in the native cyclic shift, proving commutation. No continuum
differential operator or imported translation representation is used.

The factorization is asserted on the specified cyclic one-cut target.
It is not a claim that every network curvature has this factorization.

### R45.5 — Native curvature modes have an odd cubic phase law and a controlled finite-count gap

Use R44's native cyclic modes `u_m(j)=Exp_Sigma(iota k_m j)` with
`k_m=2 theta_- m/N`. Their exchange-cost and curvature readouts are

\[
\boxed{g_m=1-\cos_\Sigma k_m,\qquad
 \omega_m=\sin_\Sigma k_m(1-\cos_\Sigma k_m).}
\tag{45.15}
\]

The curvature flow multiplies the mode by `Exp_Sigma(iota t omega_m)`.
Near zero its signed phase rate obeys

\[
\boxed{\omega(k)=\tfrac12 k^3-\tfrac18 k^5+O(|k|^7),\qquad
 \tfrac12 k^3-\tfrac18 k^5\le\omega(k)\le\tfrac12 k^3
 \quad(0\le k\le1).}
\tag{45.16}
\]

The kernel of Omega_N is the constant mode, together with the alternating
mode when N is even. Its dimension is one for odd N and two for even N.
The least nonzero absolute curvature phase rate is

\[
\boxed{\sigma_N=\sin_\Sigma(2\vartheta_-/N)
                   [1-\cos_\Sigma(2\vartheta_-/N)],\qquad
 \frac{N^3\sigma_N}{4\vartheta_-^3}\longrightarrow1.}
\tag{45.17}
\]

For N>=8 the ratio lies in `[1-16/N^2,1]`. This is distance from zero
to the nonzero curvature spectrum of the chosen finite target, not
the separation of all neighbouring eigenvalues or a physical mass gap.

**Proof.** T acts on u_m by `Exp_Sigma(-iota k_m)`. Substitution in
R44's G_N and (45.14) gives (45.15). The earned finite geometric matching
from R44 proves these modes form a complete native basis, so no extra
spectral premise is introduced. The zero factors in (45.15) give the
stated kernel, including its nonconstant alternating member for even N.

The circle addition law gives `omega(k)=sin_Sigma(k)-sin_Sigma(2k)/2`.
Its linear coefficient cancels. For j>=1 the remaining odd coefficient
is `(-1)^(j+1)(4^j-1)/(2j+1)!`. At 0<=k<=1 successive magnitudes decrease
at least by the first ratio 1/4 and then smaller bounds. Grouping the
native alternating terms proves (45.16), its remainder and the scaling
bound, since theta_-<4.

To check the minimum in (45.17), differentiate the earned circle
expression: its sign on (0,theta_-) is the sign of
`(1-cos k)(1+2 cos k)`. Thus omega rises to 2 theta_-/3 and then falls.
Reflection gives the negative half of the circle. The least positive
sample must therefore be the first or last nonzero sample in the
positive half. For even N, the last is theta_--2 theta_-/N, whose value
is no smaller than the first because cos(2 theta_-/N)>=0. For odd N,
put delta=theta_-/N and c=cos(delta). Comparing the last value at
theta_--delta with the first at 2delta reduces, after division by
sin(delta)>0, to
`1+c-4c(1-c^2)=4(c+1)(c-1/2)^2>=0`. This proves the claimed minimum.
At N=3 the two endpoint values agree. These are native circle and count
identities, without a fitted dispersion law.

The parameter t is the quadratic loop count of R45.2. Comparing this
cubic rate to R44's quadratic exchange rate does not equate their
control-depth costs or assign either a physical speed.

### R45.6 — Cost and curvature reconstruct direction while leaving full-state coherence distinct

On the cyclic one-cut target let P_const be the all-ones matrix divided
by N, and define the native matching inverse on its complement:

\[
 G_N^{\#}=(G_N+P_{\rm const})^{-1}(I-P_{\rm const}).
\tag{45.18}
\]

It exists by native finite elimination and the R44 zero-cost theorem.
Then

\[
\boxed{\Omega_NG_N^{\#}=D_N,\qquad
 T^{-1}=I-G_N+\iota\Omega_NG_N^{\#}.}
\tag{45.19}
\]

For a single cyclic mode, its ordered pair of readings (g,omega) recovers
its phase label exactly:

\[
\boxed{z=\begin{cases}
1,&g=0,\\
1-g+\iota\omega/g,&g>0.
\end{cases}}
\tag{45.20}
\]

Cost alone loses the orientation k versus -k. Curvature alone misses
the distinction between constant and alternating modes on even rings.
The pair repairs the mode-label target and reconstructs the full shift
from the two response operators. Two expectation values are not claimed
to reconstruct an arbitrary state.

**Proof.** R44 gives `ker G_N=ran P_const`. Its orthogonal complement
is invariant. Adding P_const makes the finite operator invertible:
it is identity on that cut and has zero kernel on the complement.
Native elimination constructs the inverse. Hence
`G_N G_N^#=I-P_const`. Also D_N P_const=0. Apply (45.14) to prove the
first identity in (45.19), and substitute the two Laurent expressions
to obtain T-inverse. In particular the singular zero-cost mode was
retained explicitly rather than divided away.

For a single mode, (45.15) gives cos k=1-g and, when g>0,
sin k=omega/g. Their native circle coordinate is (45.20). If g=0,
R44's connected zero-mode theorem gives z=1. Distinct native roots
therefore have distinct ordered pairs. The separate ambiguities follow
from the reflection identities and the even-ring alternating mode.

To demonstrate the full-state boundary, take two orthogonal normalized
cyclic modes v_0,v_1. The vectors `(v_0+v_1)/sqrt(2)` and
`(v_0-v_1)/sqrt(2)` have identical readouts for every polynomial in the
commuting operators G_N and Omega_N: mode cross terms vanish, and both
diagonal weights are 1/2. Their local matching profiles can differ;
the four-ledger native-circle witness checks this exactly. Thus even
all such commuting moments do not recover the omitted cross-mode
coherence. The operator-reconstruction theorem and a state-observer
theorem are distinct targets.

 \chi(x)=2\int_0^{x/2}\frac{ds}{1+s^2},\qquad

\[
\boxed{\iota(ab)\frac{d\psi}{ds}=E_{\rm phys}\psi.}

The common a,b and a material length per ledger remain unselected.
