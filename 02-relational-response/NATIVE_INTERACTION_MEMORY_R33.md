# R33: interaction memory after unresolved native continuation

Research owner: Monty Dabas. Development: 1 October 2026.

The proposed mnemonic is **alpha: memory of an interaction after its internal
continuations are unresolved**. This is a research question, not a physical
identification. R33 makes one precise version of it from R32's already
derived signed increment. No electromagnetic action, charge normalization,
ordinary complex starting field, probability law or empirical constant is
supplied. The mnemonic about pi, e and i is motivation; it is not used as a
proof of any of those constants.

For two elementary cuts in one connected response component, the derived
one-way coefficient and round-trip amplitude are

\[
r_1=3-2\sqrt2,\qquad
\boxed{\alpha_{\rm rt}=r_1^2=17-12\sqrt2}.
\tag{33.0}
\]

This is also the one-way **squared-norm** transfer fraction. The squared-norm
fraction of the complete round trip is \(\alpha_{\rm rt}^2\), not
\(\alpha_{\rm rt}\). A different, equally well-defined question, the
nonlocal fraction of a point-loaded completed response, gives exactly
\(1/9\). These are fixed consequences of specified native observations;
neither is identified with the electromagnetic fine-structure constant.

## Written results

### R33.1 — Native completion gives exact cut elimination without a new energy premise

Set \(C=E^\dagger G^{-1}E\). It obeys
\(I/4\le C\le I\), is invertible, and the unique minimizing field is

\[
\Phi_u=G^{-1}EC^{-1}u,\qquad
\min_{E^\dagger\Phi=u}\|F\Phi\|^2
 =\langle u,C^{-1}u\rangle.
\tag{33.3}
\]

For a specified load, \(\Phi_j=G^{-1}Ej\) has retained values \(u=Cj\).
Eliminating values in stages gives the same minimum as eliminating them at
once. Thus a finite unresolved interior needs no independent coupling law.

**Proof.** R32's positive difference-square identities give
\(\|Fv\|^2\in[\|v\|^2,4\|v\|^2]\) and its completed inverse gives
\(G^{-1}\). The inverse bounds need no spectral theorem. Set
\(z=G^{-1}v\). The gap and the native sum-of-squares inequality give
\(\|z\|^2\le\langle z,Gz\rangle\le\|z\|\|v\|\), hence
\(\|z\|\le\|v\|\) and
\(\langle v,G^{-1}v\rangle\le\|v\|^2\).
The upper norm bound on \(F\) also bounds its dagger by two, by the
same native pairing inequality. Therefore
\(\|v\|^2=\|F^\dagger Fz\|^2\le4\|Fz\|^2
=4\langle v,G^{-1}v\rangle\). This proves
\(I/4\le G^{-1}\le I\). Compression by the isometry \(E\) gives the
same bounds on \(C\). Finite native elimination therefore constructs
\(C^{-1}\).

The proposed \(\Phi_u\) has retained values \(u\). Any other admissible
field is \(\Phi_u+z\), with \(E^\dagger z=0\). Expand the increment
norm using the native pairing. Its cross term is zero because
\(G\Phi_u=EC^{-1}u\). Exactly,

\[
\|F(\Phi_u+z)\|^2
 =\langle u,C^{-1}u\rangle+\|Fz\|^2.
\tag{33.4}
\]

The gap makes equality unique. All quantities exist as the R32 Cauchy
limits, and the identity extends by their norm bounds. For nested sets of
retained cuts, minimizing first with the larger set and then releasing its
extra values minimizes over precisely the same fields; uniqueness proves
the claim about stages. A finite block version follows by the same square
expansion. The operator has been obtained from source evolution before this
observation is declared, not posited as an electromagnetic Hessian.

### R33.2 — Two retained cuts determine a preparation-independent round-trip invariant

Let \(p,q\) be distinct addresses in one parity component, with
\(q-p=(2a,2b)\), \(\ell=|a|+|b|\ge1\), and \(r=\rho^\ell\).
Every matrix entry below multiplies the four-role identity. Then

\[
C_{pq}=\frac12\begin{pmatrix}I&rI\\rI&I\end{pmatrix},\qquad
K_{pq}=C_{pq}^{-1}
 =\frac{2}{1-r^2}\begin{pmatrix}I&-rI\\-rI&I\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{33.5}
\]

Holding the first cut at \(u\) and releasing the second minimizes at
\(v=ru\). The reverse transfer is \(u=rv\), so their composition is
\(r^2I\). The normalized off-diagonal magnitude of \(K_{pq}\) is
\(r\), independent of the overall scale of \(G\). Therefore

\[
\alpha_{\rm rt}(p,q)=\rho^{2\ell},\qquad
\frac{\|ru\|^2}{\|u\|^2}=\alpha_{\rm rt},\qquad
\frac{\|r^2u\|^2}{\|u\|^2}=\alpha_{\rm rt}^2.
\tag{33.6}
\]

These hold for every nonzero four-role preparation. Local changes of native
pairing-preserving role frames conjugate the transfers but leave the
round-trip scalar and these norm ratios unchanged. Cuts in different parity
components have zero transfer in this Gram observation.

**Proof.** Read the two diagonal coefficients and the displacement
coefficient in (33.2). Direct multiplication proves (33.5). Its quadratic
form has the exact square completion

\[
\langle(u,v),K_{pq}(u,v)\rangle
 =2\|u\|^2+\frac{2}{1-r^2}\|v-ru\|^2.
\tag{33.7}
\]

This proves both the release map and uniqueness. Reversing the two cuts
gives its dagger map. Under frames \(U_p,U_q\), the forward map is
\(rU_q^\dagger U_p\); its reverse product is \(r^2I\). Rescaling
\(G\) by any positive scalar multiplies \(K\) by that scalar, which
cancels from its off-diagonal/self-diagonal ratio. Arbitrary port coordinate
rescaling must also transport the matching pairing; a ratio of raw entries
with the pairing discarded is not invariant. Finally (33.2) has no odd
displacement coefficient, proving the parity statement. Positive-root cuts
give the certified decimal enclosure
\(0.02943725152285<\alpha_{\rm rt}(\ell=1)<0.02943725152287\).

### R33.3 — Repeated unresolved round trips have a fixed feedback series and error

Normalize the two retained equations by their common diagonal in (33.5),
and denote their declared right-hand sides by \(s,t\). They become

\[
u-rv=s,\qquad v-ru=t,\qquad
u=\frac{s+rt}{1-\alpha_{\rm rt}}.
\tag{33.8}
\]

Eliminating the second cut once gives
\(u=s+rt+\alpha_{\rm rt}u\). Summing through \(N\) closed returns gives

\[
u_N=\sum_{k=0}^N\alpha_{\rm rt}^k(s+rt),\qquad
\|u-u_N\|
 \le\frac{\alpha_{\rm rt}^{N+1}}{1-\alpha_{\rm rt}}\|s+rt\|.
\tag{33.9}
\]

Thus the denominator is a completed count of repeated response returns, with
each coefficient fixed. This static elimination does not assign a physical
time or claim that the actual source performs this relaxation algorithm.

**Proof.** Substitute the second equation into the first. Multiply the
finite scalar sum by \(1-\alpha_{\rm rt}\); all internal terms cancel,
leaving \(1-\alpha_{\rm rt}^{N+1}\). Since
\(0<\alpha_{\rm rt}\le\rho^2<1/25\), its native Cauchy tail vanishes
and gives (33.8)--(33.9). The same calculation holds in local frames, since
the closed transfer remains \(\alpha_{\rm rt}I\). No geometric series
is assigned the meaning of probabilities, photon exchanges or a field
coupling expansion. Here its terms count repeated substitution of the
derived response relation.

### R33.4 — Releasing intermediate cuts derives a multiplicative memory law

For three ordered cuts along a count path with no coordinate backtracking,
write \(a=\rho^{\ell_{01}}\), \(b=\rho^{\ell_{12}}\). The normalized
response matrix is

\[
2C_{012}=\begin{pmatrix}1&a&ab\\a&1&b\\ab&b&1\end{pmatrix}\otimes I.
\tag{33.10}
\]

Its inverse quadratic form is

\[
\langle u,C_{012}^{-1}u\rangle
=2\|u_0\|^2+\frac{2\|u_1-au_0\|^2}{1-a^2}
 +\frac{2\|u_2-bu_1\|^2}{1-b^2}.
\tag{33.11}
\]

Release \(u_1\). The remaining two-cut form is exactly (33.5) with
\(r=ab\). In particular, keeping only every \(m\)-th elementary address
in a component gives

\[
r_m=\rho^m,\qquad
\alpha_m=\alpha_1^m,\qquad \alpha_{m+n}=\alpha_m\alpha_n.
\tag{33.12}
\]

Within \(0<\alpha_1<1\), this is not invariant under nontrivial changes
of retained address spacing. A geometric loop that backtracks cannot be
treated as a shortest-path factorization by this identity.

**Proof.** Each entry in (33.10) is a coefficient of (33.2); the no-
backtracking condition is exactly the additivity of the two count lengths.
Expand (33.11) and multiply its coefficient matrix by (33.10)/2 to get
the identity. The middle stationary value, obtained by completing its
square, is

\[
u_1=\frac{a(1-b^2)u_0+b(1-a^2)u_2}{1-a^2b^2}.
\]

Substitution gives \(2(\|u_0\|^2+\|u_2\|^2-2ab\langle u_0,u_2\rangle)
/(1-a^2b^2)\). This is also the uniqueness-under-stages result of R33.1.
Finite induction proves (33.12). If one requires
\(\alpha_1=\alpha_1^2\), the positive native field law gives only zero
or one, neither equal to the derived value. This excludes a nonzero
resolution-independent interpretation of this particular two-cut transfer;
it does not prove a general impossibility of a physical alpha selection.
For example, distinct addresses at component counts 0,2,1 have successive
lengths 2,1 but endpoint length 1, so that the shortest endpoint coefficient
is \(\rho\), not \(\rho^3\).

### R33.5 — The complete unresolved spatial response retains an exact nonlocal fraction

For a point load \(j\ne0\), set \(\Phi=G^{-1}E_0j\). Then

\[
\|\Phi\|^2=\frac9{32}\|j\|^2,\qquad
\|E_0^\dagger\Phi\|^2=\frac14\|j\|^2,\qquad
\|F\Phi\|^2=\frac12\|j\|^2.
\tag{33.13}
\]

The fraction of the response norm stored away from the loaded cut is

\[
\boxed{\chi_{\rm spread}
 =\frac{\|\Phi\|^2-\|E_0^\dagger\Phi\|^2}{\|\Phi\|^2}
 =\frac19}.
\tag{33.14}
\]

It is independent of the prepared role and of an overall positive scale of
\(G\). It is a different observable from a two-cut round-trip amplitude.

**Proof.** In component count coordinates, (33.2) gives
\(\Phi(a,b)=\rho^{|a|+|b|}j/2\). Finite symmetric count sums and their
geometric tails give

\[
\sum_{a\in\mathbb Z}\rho^{2|a|}
 =\frac{1+\rho^2}{1-\rho^2}
 =\frac3{2\sqrt2}.
\]

The last equality follows by substituting \(\rho=3-2\sqrt2\), or from
\(1+\rho^2=6\rho\) and \(1-\rho^2=4\sqrt2\rho\).
The squared two-direction sum is \(9/8\), so multiplication by the kernel's
\(1/4\) gives \(9/32\). The coefficient at the origin is \(1/2\),
giving \(1/4\). Finally \(G\Phi=E_0j\), so the native pairing gives
\(\|F\Phi\|^2=\langle\Phi,E_0j\rangle=\|j\|^2/2\).
These are three different norm questions; their distinction is necessary
for (33.14). Positive scaling cancels in the ratio, as do local orthogonal
role frames and the load norm. No Born or probabilistic postulate is used.

### R33.6 — Finite native sums certify the completed coefficient and boundary dependence

Set \(A_i=(T_i^2+T_i^{-2})/2\) and use the rational native polynomials

\[
P_{i,N}=\frac23\sum_{k=0}^N(A_i/3)^k,\quad
C_N=E^\dagger P_{y,N}P_{x,N}E,\quad
\varepsilon_N=2\,3^{-N-1}+3^{-2N-2}.
\tag{33.15}
\]

For two distinct cuts in one component,

\[
\|C_N-C\|\le\varepsilon_N,\qquad
\|C_N^{-1}-C^{-1}\|
 \le\frac{\varepsilon_N}{(2/5)(2/5-\varepsilon_N)}\quad(N\ge1).
\tag{33.16}
\]

For elementary separation, write the scalar two-cut entries as diagonal
\(a_N\), off-diagonal \(b_N\), and \(r_N=b_N/a_N\). Then

\[
|r_N-\rho|\le d_N:=\frac{(6/5)\varepsilon_N}{1/2-\varepsilon_N},
\qquad
|r_N^2-\alpha_1|\le d_N(2/5+d_N)\quad(N\ge1).
\tag{33.17}
\]

Finite cyclic boundaries must instead use wrapped kernel coefficients. On
a component cycle of length \(L\), the one-direction inverse coefficient is

\[
h_L(n)=\frac1{\sqrt2}
 \frac{\rho^n+\rho^{L-n}}{1-\rho^L},\quad 0\le n<L.
\tag{33.18}
\]

Two-dimensional coefficients are products of these. A cycle of length two
has neighboring normalized transfer \(1/3\), hence round-trip coefficient
\(1/9\); it is not the unwrapped elementary value in (33.0).

**Proof.** Finite multiplication gives
\(P_{i,N}=Q_i^{-1}(I-E_i)\), where
\(E_i=(A_i/3)^{N+1}\) has norm at most \(3^{-N-1}\).
Expanding the product and using \(\|G^{-1}\|\le1\) gives the first
bound. From (33.5) and \(r\le\rho<1/5\), the cut matrix obeys
\(C\ge(2/5)I\); equivalently, complete its plus/minus cut squares.
Since \(\varepsilon_1=19/81<2/5\), the same square estimate gives
\(C_N\ge(2/5-\varepsilon_N)I\). Its inverse is finite elimination,
with the displayed norm bound, and
\(C_N^{-1}-C^{-1}=C_N^{-1}(C-C_N)C^{-1}\) proves (33.16).
Entry errors are at most \(\varepsilon_N\). Subtract
\(r_N-\rho=(b_N-\rho a_N)/a_N\), use the exact limiting entries
\(1/2,\rho/2\), \(\rho<1/5\), and
\(a_N\ge1/2-\varepsilon_N>0\). Factoring the difference of squares
proves (33.17). These bounds are conservative but uniform in the prepared
source and contain no fitted decay.

For (33.18), wrap the positive absolutely summable R32 one-direction
kernel, grouping the integer addresses congruent to \(n\). Two finite
geometric sums and their tails give the formula, also at \(n=0\).
Multiplication by the cyclic \(Q\) gives the identity. For \(L=2\),
\(Q=(3/2)I-S/2\), \(S^2=I\), hence
\(Q^{-1}=3I/4+S/4\). The normalized off-diagonal ratio is \(1/3\).
Both the cyclic and unwrapped constructions have a native proof; silently
exchanging their boundary targets would give a false constant selection.

### R33.7 — Role, aperture and loop tests delimit what this memory coefficient selects

Full-role transfer is preparation-independent, but a single signed channel
reading \(w\) after preparing \(v\) measures the fraction

\[
\rho^{2\ell}
 \frac{|\langle w,U_q^\dagger U_pv\rangle|^2}{\|w\|^2\|v\|^2},
\tag{33.19}
\]

which can be zero or \(\rho^{2\ell}\). Spatial aperture selection changes
the invariant too. On one component line retain the normalized disjoint
averages of addresses \(\{0,1\}\) and \(\{2,3\}\), with the same four
roles. Their normalized transfer is

\[
r_{\rm av}=\frac{\rho(1+\rho)}2,
\qquad \alpha_{\rm av}=\frac{\rho^2(1+\rho)^2}{4}\ne\rho^2.
\tag{33.20}
\]

Moreover these inverse-Gram transfer maps do not reproduce the signed
record holonomy \(-I\). Their product around any finite sequence of cuts
in one component is a positive scalar times the identity, with all local
frame factors canceling. Thus R33 derives a scalar residue of R32's retained
interaction; it has not reconstructed the complete oriented record loop
from that scalar, or identified a unique electromagnetic coupling.

**Proof.** Apply the native matching readout to the R33.2 transfer. Native
sum-of-squares bounds the fraction in (33.19) by one. Choose \(w\) parallel
or perpendicular to the transported preparation for its endpoints; native
roles already supply both examples. For (33.20), compress (33.2) by the
two orthonormal aperture columns. Each diagonal entry is
\((1+\rho)/2\); the cross entry is
\(\rho(1+\rho)^2/4\), the sum of distances 2,3,1,2 with the two
normalizing factors. Divide these entries and use the same positive two-cut
square completion. Since \(0<\rho<1\), the result differs from the point
cut coefficient. These averages use derived native square roots, not a
new probability interface.

Finally every nonzero cut transfer has the form
\(\rho^{\ell_{pq}}U_q^\dagger U_p\). Ordered composition around a
closed cut list telescopes the frames, leaving
\(\rho^{\sum\ell_{pq}}I\). It cannot be \(-I\), whereas the reciprocal
record plaquette in R32.1 is exactly \(-I\). The two objects answer
different questions: eliminating a squared increment removes information
present in the signed evolution. Equations (33.12), (33.18), (33.19) and
(33.20) give explicit native counterexamples to selecting a unique physical
constant from the phrase "memory of interaction" alone. Additional native
theorems would have to select the physical observation and identify its
meaning. This is a remaining task, not permission to supply a classical
electromagnetic normalization and label it derived.
